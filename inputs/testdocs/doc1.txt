REASON FOR REFERRAL
<PERSON> was referred for a comprehensive psychological evaluation due to concerns from parents and teachers regarding difficulties with attention, emotional regulation, social interactions, and academic engagement. Symptoms have been present for approximately two years and are impacting school performance and family functioning. Parents requested diagnostic clarification and recommendations for support. Recently, <PERSON> has demonstrated increasing reluctance to attend school, often complaining of stomach aches or crying clinging to her mother during morning drop-offs.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION
Developmental History
Pregnancy, delivery, and early developmental milestones were reported as typical and within normal limits. <PERSON> sat unsupported at 7 months, walked at 13 months, used single words by 12 months, and began combining words by 20 months. Sleep patterns are irregular; <PERSON> frequently expresses fear of sleeping alone, insisting a parent stay in the room until she falls asleep. She occasionally experiences nightmares about being lost. Appetite is generally good, though she is described as a "picky eater" with specific texture aversions. 

Medical History
No major illnesses, injuries, or hospitalizations. Routine vision and hearing screenings are within normal limits. No current medications. No history of seizures, significant head trauma, or toxic exposures. <PERSON> has a history of frequent pediatrician visits for somatic complaints (headaches, stomach aches) which typically coincide with school days or periods of separation from her primary caregivers.

Family History
<PERSON> lives with both biological parents who are married. There is a reported family history of attention and anxiety-related difficulties on the paternal side; her paternal uncle was diagnosed with ADHD in childhood, and her paternal grandmother has a history of generalized anxiety. No other significant psychiatric or developmental disorders noted in immediate family. The family environment is described as supportive with consistent routines, though parents report feeling "burnt out" and exhausted from managing <PERSON>'s intense emotional needs and separation distress.

Educational History
<PERSON> attends a general education 2nd-grade classroom in a public school. Teachers report challenges with sustaining attention during group and independent work, difficulty completing assignments on time, and occasional emotional outbursts when frustrated. She often requires physical proximity to the teacher to remain on task and frequently asks for reassurance ("Is this right?"). Academic skills appear to be in the average range when structure is provided, though progress is inconsistent due to inattention and disorganization. During unstructured times like recess, <PERSON> tends to shadow the recess monitor rather than engaging in independent play with peers.

<PERSON> has a small group of friends but often has trouble sharing or managing disappointment during play. For example, if she loses a board game, she may flip the board and refuse to play again for several days. At home, she can be argumentative about daily routines and homework, often requiring intense parental scaffolding to initiate tasks. No history of aggression toward others or destruction of property. Reported strengths include a vivid imagination, creativity (particularly in drawing and storytelling), kindness toward younger children, and strong expressive language skills.

BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATIONS
<PERSON> arrived on time and was initially shy but warmed up quickly. She was talkative and cooperative yet displayed frequent fidgeting, shifting in her seat, and occasional off-task behaviors. Attention to tasks varied; she required moderate redirection. For example, during block design tasks, she would begin stacking blocks to build a "castle" rather than replicating the stimulus pattern. Speech was clear and fluent. Affect was generally euthymic with some anxiety noted during challenging tasks, manifesting as lip-biting and asking to see her mother in the waiting room. Effort and motivation appeared adequate throughout the evaluation sessions.

TEST RESULTS AND CLINICAL FINDINGS
Cognitive Ability
On the WISC-V, <PERSON>’s overall intellectual functioning fell in the Average to High Average range (Full Scale IQ = 112, 79th percentile). Strongest performance was observed in Verbal Comprehension and Fluid Reasoning, indicating excellent ability to understand verbal concepts and solve novel problems. Processing Speed and Working Memory indices were significantly lower relative to other domains, falling in the low average range. This discrepancy is highly consistent with reported attentional concerns and suggests <PERSON> is expending a high degree of cognitive effort simply to maintain focus.

Attention and Executive Functioning
Results from the Conners 4 and TOVA indicated clinically significant difficulties with inattention and impulsivity in both home and school environments (T-scores ranging from 68–76). Performance on the TOVA showed elevated omission and commission errors along with high variability in response time, supporting deficits in sustained attention and inhibitory control. Parent reports highlight significant deficits in task initiation and working memory, noting she often forgets multi-step directions.

Social-Emotional and Behavioral Functioning
BASC-3 profiles revealed clinically significant elevations in hyperactivity, attention problems, and emotional self-regulation across reporters. SCARED scores indicated elevated anxiety symptoms (parent total score = 28, above clinical cutoff), particularly related to separation and school performance. <PERSON> self-reported significant worry about "bad things happening to mom and dad." CDI-2 results did not suggest significant depressive symptoms. Teacher ratings were somewhat higher than parent ratings, indicating greater impairment in the structured and demanding school setting.

Diagnostic Impression
Based on a review of developmental history, behavioral observations, multi-informant standardized measures, and cognitive testing, <PERSON> meets DSM-5-TR criteria for the following diagnoses:
1. Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Combined Presentation (F90.2)
Moderate severity. Symptoms are present across multiple settings and cause clinically significant impairment in academic and social functioning.
2. Separation Anxiety Disorder (F93.0)
Mild severity. Excessive fear of separation from attachment figures that interferes with daily activities, notably school attendance and independent sleeping.

No evidence of a specific learning disorder, autism spectrum disorder, or oppositional defiant disorder was identified at this time. Notable strengths include solid verbal abilities and a supportive family system.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Medical Consultation – Referral to a child and adolescent psychiatrist for consideration of medication management options for ADHD symptoms, which may concurrently lower the baseline arousal driving her anxiety.
Behavioral Interventions – Participation in evidence-based parent training program (e.g., 8–12 sessions of behavioral parent management training) to address compliance, establish structured morning routines, and manage separation distress systematically.
School Supports – Request a Section 504 Plan or IEP meeting to implement classroom accommodations. Recommended accommodations include preferential seating near the teacher, extended time on assignments/tests, frequent scheduled movement breaks, visual schedules, and a "safe pass" to visit the school counselor when feeling overwhelmed. Daily behavior report card recommended.
Individual Therapy – Weekly cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) focused on anxiety management, developing a "bravery ladder" for separation scenarios, coping skills, and building emotional regulation. Services can be coordinated through <ORGANIZATION> or community providers.
Extracurricular Activities – Enroll <PERSON> in a structured, non-competitive group activity (e.g., art class or theater) to build self-esteem and practice social interactions in a low-stakes environment.
Follow-up – Re-evaluation in 12 months or earlier if significant changes occur. Ongoing monitoring of academic progress and response to interventions is advised.

Summary Statement
<PERSON> is a bright and engaging 8-year-old girl presenting with ADHD, Combined Presentation and co-occurring Separation Anxiety Disorder. Her cognitive profile reveals significant strengths in verbal and fluid reasoning undermined by vulnerabilities in processing speed and working memory. With timely, evidence-based intervention including behavioral support, targeted school accommodations, and CBT therapy, significant improvement in attention, anxiety, and overall functioning is highly anticipated.
